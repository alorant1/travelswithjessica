Marrakech, Morocco

Marrakech, Morocco December, 2018 Sights, Sounds and Smells of Marrakech After a 32 hour trip (including two 6 hour delays…don’t get me started on baggage transit efficiency), we made it to Marrakech! The sales tactics are different than the Souk in Jerusalem. It’s gone from “you will be my first sale of the day; you will bring me good luck” to “no Madame, it is closed that way, you must come this way”. One such diversion led us to a small, 15-family tannery cooperative. They kindly gave us a “Berber gas mask” which is a fistful of fresh mint… Fascinating process, all manual and all natural; who knew there was a market for pigeon poo! Missed opportunity at Granville Island. Oh and Adam is doing a great job making new friends! Now what does Father Christmas have in his bag for Alex? I’ve tried for years to get him to wear a scarf! I think granddaddy may not be happy with my treatment of baby. Midday sun behind Koutoubia Mosque. Kind of like jumping out of a birthday cake… Shoes, glorious shoes! Ain’t no flies on us! Adam made a new friend today! Previous Next The Medina Palaces, riads, gardens and cats. Crisscrossing the Médina, we explored the fancy and not-so-fancy stomping grounds of Moroccans past and present. We hired a guide to take us around the nooks and crannies of the Médina: astonishing how he navigates the jumble of streets and pathways, not to mention the motorcycles, scooters, bicycles and donkey carts competing for space in the narrow alleyways. Oh, and a few strategic visits to “special” artist cooperatives selling carpets, furniture, jewelry and argan oil…Capped off with dinner in Jemma el Fna square which transforms itself from snake charmers and acrobats by day to a hundred food stalls by night. The waiters and chefs call out to attract you to their stalls and when a customer falls prey, they all clap and sing a welcome song. Thanks to Alex’s friend, Zaiq, for the recommendation. Amazing that we actually found it: “117 takes you to heaven”! Line up at the fish shop. Moroccan rainbow. Mood lighting Le Jardin Secret…not so secret anymore! At least they are honest with their customers. Photobomb at the Jardin Secret. Wish we could buy them all! The rooms of the Grand Vizier of the Sultan’s 24 concubines in the Bahia Palace (named for his favourite wife, who clearly had a separate space). Not quite the Sultan yet…but give him time. Inside the Museum of Moroccan Art and Handicrafts, housed in the architecturally-stunning home of Si Said. An amazing collection of the culturally all-important Berber and Arab carpets. Pointy shoes! The jemma el fna transforms at night from acrobats and snake charmers to a hundred food stalls. Thanks to Alex’s friend Zaiq for the recommendation:”117 takes you to heaven!” Previous Next Old Town to New Town and the Perfect Blue Today was spent exploring sharp contrasts in Marrakech. A leisurely walk took us from the Médina (Old Town) to the Guéliz (the New Town) and the Majorelle Gardens. A French painter, Jacques Majorelle, bought the huge palm grove in 1923 and designed the gardens around his studio which he painted in Majorelle Blue. After he died, it was abandoned and slated for demolition until Yves-Saint-Laurent saved the day by buying and renovating the gardens to what we see today. A quiet oasis in the hustle and bustle of the city with stunning colours and unbeatable style. Shoe parts in the Médina. A giant leap from the traditional Médina stalls but still in the Médina. Max & Jan is a lifestyle store; a modern riff on traditional clothes, accessories and furniture. Very cool space. We even went back for dinner on the roof! Now here’s a unique Christmas tree… The colours of the Majorelle gardens. Majorelle blue. Completed shoes…YSL style! Couldn’t resist another carpet shot… School pick up time. Rooftop dinner at Max & Jan. Goodnight! Ouarzazate, Morocco

Ouarzazate, Morocco December, 2018 Desert-bound Today was mind-blowing! We began our desert adventure at 8am with the most wonderful guide, Mubarak. So far, we have passed through the high Atlas Mountains, reaching a peak in this area of 2260m (snow peaks in the distance. In January/February there will be enough snow to ski). We visited an argan oil women’s cooperative where we learned how argan oil is made. We stopped at a few Kasbahs (home/fort of the local leader, often built high on a hill for defense) on the way. One of the Kasbahs was owned by the “salt lord”, El Glaoui, and was situated on the trading route from the south. The traders passed through in search of salt in exchange for gold (equally valuable at the time). This guy was the main game en route and amassed a great fortune. It took 300 people three years to build his Kasbah (equivalent to $45 million labour cost in today’s dollars), which seriously rivals Vancouver’s real estate market! The Ksar at Ait-Ben-Haddou is a Berber castle located strategically along the main trade route between Sudan and the imperial cities of Morocco. Location, location, location! This ended up the site of many an epic movie location such as Laurence of Arabia, Gladiator and Cleopatra to name a few. We are now nestled in between the high and low Atlas Mountains at the most stunning Riad and will continue the journey to the Sahara tomorrow. First mountain stop, Touama. Sea-to-Sky Highway, desert-style. Heading home. Kasbah Telouet. Three Kasbahs on one site. One built in the 1700s out of mud, then abandoned for a new one built in the 1800s out of bricks. We toured though the third built in the 1920s. this last one was abandoned in 1956 when the French left Morocco (the descendants of the El Galoui family were French sympathizers). The “modern” Kasbah Telouet. Beautiful mosaics in the Kasbah. Mubarak at the Kasbah. Alex started an archeological dig when he noticed an interesting object buried in the salt flats in Telouet. He stuck in his thumb and pulled out a dog’s jaw! Lots and lots of Berber villages camouflaged in the mountains. The Ksar at Ait-Ben-Haddou. Goodnight from Riad Ksar Ighnda in Ait Ben Haddou. Zagora, Morocco

Zagora, Morocco December, 2018 Erg Chegaga Only accessible by foot, camel or 4×4 (we did it all, but the longest part was by 4×4), Erg Chegaga is the largest dunes in the Moroccan Sahara (40km x 15km, 60m high). This place is really far (maybe 400km each way), a gruelling journey but well worth it. The camp is 30km from the Algerian border (some police checkpoints entering and exiting the desert). It was a 10 hour drive back (with stops for lunch and to buy a carpet…more on that in a moment), but one doesn’t go very fast in the sand. These guys who drive on the desert sand would put any North American winter driver to shame. The desert is a remarkable place. The shapes, curves, angles and colours are unlike anything else. We rode camels to the highest dune, parked our beasts and climbed up to watch the sunset. Stunning. After dinner, they lit a campfire and entertained us with songs and drumming. What an amazing experience. We did wonder if it was all “just for the tourists” but this morning we happened upon the group making and drinking tea together at their morning fire outside the camp: It’s their regular thing. And they invited us to share in their tea!  We stopped at a women’s carpet cooperative. This cooperative makes all the raw materials. They have their own sheep and camels from which they shear, spin and dye the wool. They work on carpets in teams of two. Carpets usually take 3 months of work, depending on size. Fatima is the elected president of the cooperative which was created many years ago in support of women’s employment and independence. Presidents are elected based on their experience, widsom and abilities. They tend to be in their 70s. 450 women from the local village make up the cooperative and Fatima manages the women, teaches skills and does final negotiation on the carpets. The government supports the cooperative as a means of employment and independence for the women. The government sets the prices and carpets are sold for the same price from the cooperative to individuals or to retailers. Small negotiation allowed, particularly if buying more than one.This whole desert journey was truly National Geographic come to life. First oasis of many we passed in the Draa Valley. This is the biggest date farming area with 30 different types of dates. A local pottery cooperative. The men do all the clay work and firing. The women paint the designs. Notice how the wheel is at ground level: all the better to be cooler in 45C heat. Finished pottery Robyn practicing for the upcoming camel ride. Sandboarding! Not as fast as on the snow. Yup, finally got Adam on a piece of fibreglass pointing down a “mountain”. The big question is will he take it to the next level: snow!
