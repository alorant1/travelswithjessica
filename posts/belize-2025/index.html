Beautiful Belize

Beautiful Belize December 2024 Precious Placencia We are wrapping up 2024 with a family trip to Belize, starting beachside in Placencia. This is a fabulous, low-key town without the tourist crush of some of the Cayes. We are now spread over two continents and three cities so these family trips bringing us together are pretty special. Just us on the beach. This beach is at the Placencia Beach Club. We thought it a bit odd that the beach club closed at 5pm. When we left and settled our tab, I commented on the bites we were getting. She said those were sand flies and that’s why they close at 5! Lesson learned…get off the beach well before dusk. A recent hurricane destroyed the little pier outside our lovely hotel. The owners took on the task of rebuilding most of it but left this part for the birds. I’m proposing this as a future book club venue. The reason we started our trip in Placencia was for Robyn, Evan and Alex to do their PADI open water dives. They are all freshly certified! While the kids were working at being safe underwater, we took to a kayak and explored the coast of Placencia and the split in Placencia Caye across the way from our hotel. Hidden from view in the split is quite a collection of very fancy properties! Not so fancy but full of Belizean charm.https://videos.files.wordpress.com/elNSP314/img_1463.movAnother bit of Belizean charm. Meet William the plastic man! He’s born and raised in Placencia and for the past 16 years he’s been doing his part keeping the beach and town clean. He calls himself a recycler. He picks up plastic bags, cuts them in strips and then stretches them into plastic strings that he makes into macrame bracelets. He’s famous! The woman who owns this wine bar has the best sign. At this moment she’s open for business… …when she’s closed, she moves the “N” to the front. The owner of the Tipsy Tuna has all sorts of inspirational messages along the path. Important tidbits like: Make today so awesome yesterday gets jealous. Nobody ever ruined their eyesight by looking at the bright side. The last mosquito that bit me was hungover for a week. Drunken words are sober thoughts. If you think you are too small to make a difference, you’ve never spent the night with a mosquito. Always be kind! The toes you step on today may be attached to the butt you have to kiss tomorrow. It’s all shits and giggles until someone giggles and shits. Beachside bar. Goodnight Placencia! Jungle, Rainforest and Mosquitoes! We’ve moved from a narrow spot in the ocean off the mainland to the Mountain Pine Ridge Forest Reserve and lush jungle not far from the Guatemalan border. Humidity and mosquitos have ramped up but at least no sand flies. The nearby town of San Ignacio has a surprising collection of must-see stops! There are over 2000 caves in Belize but exploring caves is a relatively new pursuit here. Our guide told us that people here are reluctant to go in them because they are full of spirits. His grandparents told him not to go inside the caves because you never know what will follow you out! The younger generation no longer believes in the spirits so in they go! Actun Tunichil Muknal (the cave of the crystal sepulchre) is reputed to be the place to visit in Belize due to its sheer size (5km deep) and important spiritual significance. It’s a huge river cave that was a sacred site for the Maya. Just before the decline of the Maya empire around 900AD, the region suffered a few hundred years of drought (which was a contributing factor to its demise). When the drought just wouldn’t let up, the Maya ventured deeper in the caves with a flurry of appeals in the form of sacrifices and offerings thinking they’d get more action from the gods, sadly to no avail. Apparently, that’s not how climate change works. It was an amazing day but no photos are allowed. In the years since they opened this cave to the public (1997) after its discovery in 1986, tourists have been distracted taking photos and have stepped on the artifacts inside. Others have dropped their camera and crushed skulls or knocked out ancient teeth. Cameras are now banned so the photos of the cave are courtesy of the Internet. Our journey to the ATM cave took us on a hike through three river crossings to reach the mouth of the cave. On the way we tasted termites! Why I asked what that black bulge on the tree was, I don’t know, but that is what prompted this culinary experience. Our guide coaxed them out of their tree home and gave us each one to try. She told us to be sure to pop them with our teeth to get the flavour (and maybe to keep them from running amok on our tongues). Alex’s tasted like mint while Robyn’s, Evan’s and mine tasted like carrot; all classic termite tasting notes. Once we got to the mouth of the cave, we swam through passages, climbed over the massive and jagged boulders from the collapsed limestone ceiling and squeezed through tight spaces. Lots of skeletons of babies, children and virgin maidens, as well as ceramic bowls from food offerings, remain in there from that time, fused to the cave floors from the millennia of dripping mineral-rich water, along with the most magical stalagmites and stalagtites. One of the highlights deep inside the cave is the Crystal Maiden, a mostly complete skeleton of a young woman covered in limestone crystals making her sparkle. They say she voluntarily offered herself up for sacrifice because she was left in a dancing pose. However post sacrifice, she had no control over her placement.
