Copenhagen, Denmark

Copenhagen, Denmark March, 2019 Amazing Art and Architecture What a remarkable city! Architecture, food, art, design, style, this place has it all… St Nicholas Church dates from the 1200s, but its iconic tower (the tallest church tower in the city) was built a few hundred years later in 1591. Less than 40 years later, a windy day brought down the spire (I guess building codes didn’t include wind protection) which wasn’t rebuilt for another 40 years. This church continued to have a fraught existence, with the spire falling down again in the 1795 fire that ravaged Copenhagen, leaving the church in ruins. The spire was lost and there was no money to rebuild the church so it was torn down with only the tower remaining. The tower had a few rebirths; as a fire watchman’s tower (ironically), as the definitive city clock where a ball dropped at 1pm every day and was finally rebuilt with its original spire that had been lost after the big fire. The surrounding building was rebuilt but never returned to its church beginnings and after serving as a naval museum and the main public library, it is now Copenhagen’s exhibition centre. Nice that 800 years later after surviving the slings and arrows of time and weather, the tower remains as a functional part of the city. Inside the kaleidoscope art installation. Faroe Islands painter and glass artist Tróndur Pattersun created this street art installation called Cosmic Space. Inspired by his experience on a sinking ship in the open sea surrounded by an infinity of dark sky and ocean. Nyhavn. Perhaps one of the most beautiful half kilometres of canal waterfront anywhere! Lines with stunning 17th and 18th century townhouses sitting atop a multitude or restaurants and bars, facing many historical wooden ships. And the ubiquitous bridge with love locks. Our very own Karate Kid From atop the Baroque Our Saviours Church. Beautiful spire with an external winding staircase all the way to the top. The views were amazing! Popular snack. What could be better than a waffle on a stick covered in warm Nutella and Oreo cookie crumbles? So many public spaces to hang out and socialize. The trampoline sidewalk, just outside our hotel. In front of the Christiansborg Palace. It is the seat of the Danish Parliament and Danish Supreme Court. The monarchy also uses parts of the palace for receptions but they don’t live there. The royal kitchen. More copper pots, and giant ones at that, than I ever imagined in one place. Not much real cooking for receptions happens here. Most is done sous vide in the actual living place and then transported here to be reheated, plated and served. They gave us shoe covers to walk around the reception rooms. I guess they stay on top of the floor sweeping this way. Thrones! So many cyclists in Copenhagen. Apparently more bicycles than cars here. Dedicated bike lanes everywhere. Even stroller parking! …and a pedestrian crosswalk for the bike lane. So much fun riding electric scooters around! The Design Museum was fantastic. An homage to the ever-present bike.
