Oahu, Hawaii

Oahu, Hawaii December, 2022 Outstanding Oahu Well, this is a long one because I didn’t think I’d have so much to talk about… Note that the food part of the story is still to come. We are not seasoned Hawaii travellers, having only visited Maui and the big island when the kids were younger. The week on Oahu flew by and was the best time we’ve had in Hawaii. Hiking, surfing, scuba and great food. No surfing photos as we were all rocking wetsuits 50 metres from shore to catch the waves. Suffice it to say it was a fantastic time and Mike from Mike’s Surf School in Kahuku on Oahu’s North Shore, is an awesome teacher. We all got vertical! Four days, seven cities and they are finally here…We beat the weather (and airlines)! Our first day, we hiked the Waimano Falls trail. Despite it being a fairly short trail (about 5km out and back), it was pretty steep in areas and required a lot of scrabbling over roots and rocks. And what a treat at the bottom!https://videos.files.wordpress.com/Qv9r3YnM/img_8646-5.movAt the bottom of the trail is the waterfall’s landing spot, a very cold pool…as the old saying goes, “it’s fine once you get in!”. The rope swing is the icing on the cake. Wardrobe change! Stunning sunsets every night. At least the dog is getting a walk… Scuba day with Big Kahuna Watersports. Greg, the owner, was amazing and took us through the basics of scuba, use of equipment and hand signals. Thirty minutes or so later, the five of us were suited up and ready to dive under Greg and another instructor’s supervision. Snorkelling is fun and you see such beauty but scuba is a whole other level. I’m so grateful we were able to experience it safely (max depth 35 ft) even though we are not certified. The kids, however, are keen to go the distance and want to plan a sibling scuba certification trip! This year’s New Year family pic! Robyn lounging on the ocean floor. Me practicing my ballet moves. Adam has fully embraced the Shaka culture of friendship, gratitude, compassion and solidarity. It’s very common here as this is a very friendly place inspiring lots of gratitude! The origins of this hand sign is debatable but local lore tells the tale of guy called Hamana Kalili who lost his three middle fingers working at the Kahuku Sugar Mill. Kalili could no longer do the same job once he recovered from his injury so he guarded the sugar train instead. His all-clear wave with his remaining thumb and pinkie is said to have inspired the Shaka. Evan getting up close and personal with a sea cucumber. I know what he is thinking…if I take it back to Halifax, will it go in my fish tank or my sandwich? The next thing you know, Greg swims over to a little gap in the rocks and and teases out a friend. After a substantial ink release, he stayed to play with us. I think he liked Robyn the best and wanted to keep her, wrapping his tentacles around her hands. So graceful.https://videos.files.wordpress.com/hA4SMyi4/gh010521-5.mp4And now it’s time to leave. Notice at the end when he slides under the rock, he’s got a buddy waiting. The next hike we attempted was the Koko Crater Rim. There were signs indicating this ridge trail was closed but people had rated it on AllTrails just a few days before so, of course, the signs must be wrong… We started with a bit of bushwhacking, the first indicator that the trail was not very well used… The trail changed very quickly and all seemed fine when we came out into the sun and continued our ascent. But the trail got narrower and the edge into the crater got closer and closer… We had to make the parental safety decision to turn around and find another hike. Second attempt was a surprising winner! We headed over to the Lighthouse Hike, apparently Obama’s favourite. It’s a paved, winding road up the hill to a lighthouse. Beautiful views and not particularly challenging. Great for kids, strollers and flip flops. The kids peered over the edge at one of the lookouts and spied tide pools way down at the bottom of the mountain so that became our winner of a hike. Making our way down the mountainside. Not great for flip flops but we did see a few people wearing such footwear and worse! The tide pool was a perfect temperature and we had it to ourselves for a while. We didn’t bring bathing suits and towels from the car as we didn’t know we would end up here. We went in wearing our clothes. How’s that for a LinkedIn profile pic? There’s the lighthouse peeking out…and some guy… Evan totally fits the vibe here.https://videos.files.wordpress.com/8H9FFU5t/e849bbf0-4de2-4532-b31c-197aac10e097-3.movA bit further along the shoreline rocks we found three blowholes. The other two shot up a fine mist occasionally but this one just seemed to shoot up air. Perfect spot to dry off…so we thought! Back to the North Shore to experience the surfing beaches and watch the pros. Sunset Beach had stunning waves but not much surfing action.https://videos.files.wordpress.com/fS1TLQRB/img_9410-3.movNext to Sunset Beach we found Banzai Pipeline where waves can be anywhere from two feet to 40 feet. It’s hard to tell how big the waves are but use the surfer in this video to get a sense of scale. A stop at Waikiki Beach on our last night. Lots of people on the beach and surfers bobbing about in the water enjoying the sunset. Aloha Oahu. Mahalo for all the amazing experiences!
