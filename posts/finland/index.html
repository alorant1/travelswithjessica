Helsinki, Finland

Helsinki, Finland March, 2019 The Unique Helsinki Vibe All together now! Very cool city (literally and figuratively). What a mix of architecture from old to new, austere to decorative. And trying to pronounce the names of places is an exercise in fun and futility. White caps like we’ve never seen before! And in the Baltic Sea no less. The Helsinki Cathedral. Watch out Granville Island…they’re coming! Oodi Central Library. This is by far the most remarkable “library”. The term library is really used quite loosely. It’s a massive public space (opened in December 2018) in the centre of the city: meeting space, creative space, hanging out space, oh and there are books too. There are serious craft areas where you can draw, use their sewing machines, 3D print, play VR games and do large format printing. We didn’t manage to find the secret door to the fairytale land…I guess it’s just that secret… Artwork incorporated into the circular stairway in the library. Nearly 400 words that locals were invited to submit describing who they felt the library was for. While yet to be translated into English, the words range from “Thinker” to “Alcoholic”. It’s really a place for everyone! Public skating at the Ice Park in Railway Square. Kamppi Chapel of Silence…curse those squeaky shoes! Previous Next Rocks, Organs and Sausages Chilly day visiting the Rock Church, Sibelius Monument and the super cute Regatta Café. Coffee partners! The amazing Temppeliaukio Church (aka the Rock Church), built right into solid rock. Stunning space with light streaming in all around. We were fortunate to be there while a pianist proved the unbelievable acoustics. Sibelius Monument to honour the Finnish composer. Interestingly it is comprised of 600 hollow steel pipes, resembling an organ but Sibelius wrote very little for the organ. It is meant to symbolize the waves of sound. Lost in the pipes Regatta Cafe. Originally built in 1887 as a shed for fishnets, it is now a quirky homage to music and all things weird and wonderful. Phew, one rental left! Buy your sausage inside the cafe and the roast it on the open fire outside. It’s cold here for the poor birds. Thankfully there is a reasonable vacancy rate… The Rock Church in full. Previous Next Suomenlinna Island A windy day circumnavigating Suomenlinna Island. Only accessible by water, the sea fortress dates back to 1748 when Finland was a part of Sweden. Its history started as a naval base for Sweden during the war between Sweden and Russia, then a naval base for Russia after the war between Russia and Finland, a POW camp during the Finnish Civil War, a submarine and anti-aircraft base during WWII, and finally a UNESCO World Heritage Site for its outstanding military architecture. Eight hundred people now live here. Well, look who we bumped into today! Boots keep seagull feet warm in the sea ice. Steppin’ out on the floes.. The sea fortress walls. Yoga…anywhere and everywhere. All in! The girls…for once in a majority! …and a chair for two to curl up in (à la Friendly Giant). Previous Next Previous PostNext Post

Lapland, Finland

Lapland, Finland March, 2019 Almost As North as North Gets Our Finnish adventure ends in Lapland. Bucket list checklist: stay in an ice hotel snowmobile 30 km across a frozen sea at 100km/hour swim in the sea ice see the Aurora Borealis A bit of ice fishing for dinner on the Gulf of Bothnia. Cross country skiing. Where? On the Gulf of Bothnia of course Cosy dinner in the Ice Hotel. Northern Friendly Giant’s chair for two to curl up in. Bedtime. Thankfully we could turn the lights off. The ice hotel provides supervision. Cheap babysitting! 100km per hour across the ice to the ice breaker. Sampo ice breaker. Taken out of service after a mere 25 years because the cargo ships grew too big for the channels Sampo could cut. Cutting through the ice. Robyn and Dani’s inner Teletubbies. Nothing like a beach vacation to take the edge off. Waterpolo anyone? Soft landing. Go local! Previous Next Previous PostNext Post
