Barcelona, Spain

Barcelona, Spain January, 2019 Oh Barcelona, how do I love thee? Let me count the ways… Gaudí, markets, music, art, food, architecture, biking along the beach, shopping, fountains and having the incomparable Joanna Miller with us! Casa Batlló, a house renovated by Antoni Gaudí at the turn of the 20th century. The attention to detail, down to custom, ergonomic door handles, is astonishing. In front of Casa Batlló. This house sure stands out on the block…I wonder if it enhances the neighbourhood’s property values… Casa Batlló is covered with colourful mosaic bits. Glass windows in Casa Balltó that look like running water. Mercat de la Boqueria leaves Granville Island in the dust. Walking through makes my mouth water most of the time however I’m happy to leave some animal parts in the display cases (notably bare sheep’s head complete with eyes…just don’t know how to cook that one!). However, peppers and garlic…with those I can cook! Mercat de Santa Caterina has the coolest roof…and the best truffle potato chips! Spanish guitar at Park Guell. The graffiti all over Barcelona is amazing! At the top of Park Guell Pinxos! Tapas! Who loves Barcelona! Whacky art and sculpture prevails! Annual family New Year’s Eve poker with M&Ms…doesn’t matter where in the world we are! Incoming! Sagrada Familia…not finished yet! Sagrada Familia Rainbow part 1 Sagrada Familia rainbow part 2 Amazing meal at ABaC. Four hour and 22 courses with wine pairings! Walking in or out? Words to live by… Can’t miss us in a crowd! Tandem bike and then some! Nightly Magic Fountain show in front of the Palau Nacional replete with music and lights. Costa Brava, Spain

Costa Brava, Spain January, 2019 Girona, Figueres and Cadaques Road trip to the medieval town of Girona, Figueres (the birthplace of Salvador Dalí) and the stunning seaside town of Cadaqués where Dalí had his home later in life.After two weeks of tajine and tapas, we are all looking forward to some kale and quinoa in LA! Beautiful Girona along the River Onyar. Feels a little like Florence. Bizarre rainbow by the cathedral in Girona. The Roman walls of Girona’s old quarter, the Barri Vel. Catalán liberation is alive and well. Who knew we’d hit another national holiday. Three Kings Day is the day when the kids receive gifts from…the Three Kings. All over the country there are parades through the streets. In Girona, the gold, frankincense and myrrh seemed to flow long past the kids’ bedtime. Keeping control of the revellers. We won’t get into political correctness of the costumes… The Pont de les Peixateries Velles, designed by Gustave Eiffel around 1877. A practice run for his more famous tower in Paris? I found a bike in Girona for my next bike trip! The Dalí Theatre and Museum in Figueres. Dalí renovated the theatre here in his hometown (after it was destroyed during the Civil War) to house the most important collection of his works. Three reasons why he chose this spot: 1) he was baptized across the street, 2) his very first art exhibition was held in thGe lobby of that theatre, and 3) given the theatrical nature of his work, he felt it was an appropriate place. Warning: there is virtually no information about the works on display, no app you can download, no audio guides, no nothing. And this work definitely could use some analysis for the lay person! So if you go, read up on it first. Beautiful Cadaques. This is a resort town I could stay in! Quite a foodie place too. Early sunset in Cadaques. This was our post-lunch walk…yes, we are on the Spanish meal schedule! Cadaqués was Dalís home for decades later in life.
