Skidegate

Taking advantage of the rain, we headed to Skidegate and the Haida Heritage Centre. This should be everyone’s first stop on a first visit to Haida Gwaii. After absorbing as much as we could about the culture here in Haida Gwaii, we popped into Ben Davidson’s gallery and got to chat with the artist himself. Nice that he sometimes collaborates with his dad, Robert Davidson. We are now tucked into the cozy Alaska View Lodge in Masset to reflect on the day.

Old Masset

From hippy culture and old boats to beautiful poles and phenomenal artists, tiny Old Masset has it all. Old Masset is one of two villages where the Haida regrouped after suffering severely declining numbers thanks to Smallpox. We were told that this little town raised an incredible number of world-class Haida artists, and we were fortunate enough to meet a few of them, all over the course of a few hours! We stumbled upon Jim Hart’s studio where he was working with his team on a a couple of remarkable and substantial commissions, we bumped into Corey Bulpitt, whose work already figures prominently in our home, and we met John Brent Bennett, an incredibly talented carver and print maker. Wow!

Moon over Naikoon: a bakery in a bus!

These young women crank a lot of deliciousness out of this tiny kitchen. Jody, on the right, is the granddaughter of Wendy who started it all. Her grandmother was in Nanaimo when her daughter (Paige) called and told her she’d love Haida Gwaii. So Wendy packed up her Corvette Stingray and drove through a snowstorm to get here. Needless to say, the Corvette got stuck in the snow so grandma unpacked everything she could carry, plus her cat, and continued on. She made it to Haida Gwaii and started bakery. She passed away four years ago and now daughter and granddaughter come up from Nanaimo every summer to keep Moon Over Naikoon rising.

Tow Hill

Lots of outdoor time today with hiking, big trees and a beautiful sunset. Oh, and one more pole to share…

The end of Tow Hill Beach has the most spectacular lava rock formations, thanks to the volcano that is now Tow Hill.

Easy to find Waldo...

Top of Tow Hill overlooking miles of beach below. No, we did not drive our little Chevy the 25 miles along Rose Spit. Apparently, every year a few cars get washed away when the tide comes in and surprises those who don’t know how to read ride tables.

Hollow log on the hike up Tow Hill. We learned that logs deteriorate from the inside out and poles that are hollowed out will stand 50-100 years longer than solid ones.

Stunning green moss on the rocks.

Christian White’s pole at Hiellen, made from a 600 year old cedar. It’s 15.5 metres tall, not to mention the additional 3.4 metres in the ground to hold it up. It was inspired by a pole created in 1820 that fronted a chief’s house for 100 years. The original pole was painted by Emily Carr in 1911.

Big trees along Golden Spruce Trail in Port Clements. The trail was originally created for people to go see the famous, 300-year-old and 50m tall unusual tree that was a symbol for many. I’m 1997, a former logger turned zealous environmental activist cut in down to protest logging. Not quite sure of the logic of that protest statement.

Really big trees. Not many first growth forests around...we met a couple in here with a drone who were filming the impact of ongoing logging. They said there is a lot of clear-cutting still happening on Haida Gwaii and they want to raise awareness to stop it.

Tlell

Sitting at the beach in Tlell, an eagle perched on a spit, dusk settling in to the east, a picnic dinner of wine, cheese and hummus. A fire on the beach, gazing at a vast network of stars to cap it all off. Can you imagine a better late summer evening? Haida Gwaii is a quiet, magical place. Grid is patchy at best and that’s a good thing. The people here are so warm and welcoming. I’ll be back…

Progressive Masset, complete with rainbow crosswalk and a real coffee shop with an espresso machine! And the flower power van from Alberta.

Walking through the woods to the Pesuta shipwreck at the end of East beach. When trees fall naturally across a maintained trail, they will cut out the section blocking the trail and leave the rest where it fell. Someone painstakingly counted the rings. This one was 150 years old.

...just sayin’...

The Pesuta shipwreck on East beach (Naikoon Provincial Park). The 264 foot log carrier ran aground in 1928. The locals used its cargo for building supplies and here’s what’s left ninety years later.

What’s left of the portholes on the Pesuka. I’ll never forget my Mom’s story as a young bride on an ocean trip with with my Dad. My Dad was quite particular about his packing...he had a system. She forgot to give him a pair of shoes to pack. Instead of batting her eyelashes and handing over the shoes (which would have resulted in a complete repack), she threw them out the porthole window.
