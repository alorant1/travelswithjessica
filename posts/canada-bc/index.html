Haida Gwaii, British Columbia

Haida Gwaii, British Columbia July, 2019 Skidegate Taking advantage of the rain, we headed to Skidegate and the Haida Heritage Centre. This should be everyone’s first stop on a first visit to Haida Gwaii. After absorbing as much as we could about the culture here in Haida Gwaii, we popped into Ben Davidson’s gallery and got to chat with the artist himself. Nice that he sometimes collaborates with his dad, Robert Davidson. We are now tucked into the cozy Alaska View Lodge in Masset to reflect on the day. Tim Boyko’s Sgang Gwaay pole at the Haida Heritage Centre. Raised in 2001, it represents one of the six villages of Skidegate. Part of Ronald Wilson’s T’aanuu pole at the Heritage Centre. Part of Jim Hart’s K’uuna pole. Inukshuk with antler at Balance Rock beach. Some playful soul installed a bunch of swings along the beach. All was well until Adam came along and pushed me high into the sky! Balance Rock. Looks like it’s raining over there. An homage to the past glory of a tree. Previous Next Old Masset From hippy culture and old boats to beautiful poles and phenomenal artists, tiny Old Masset has it all. Old Masset is one of two villages where the Haida regrouped after suffering severely declining numbers thanks to Smallpox. We were told that this little town raised an incredible number of world-class Haida artists, and we were fortunate enough to meet a few of them, all over the course of a few hours! We stumbled upon Jim Hart’s studio where he was working with his team on a a couple of remarkable and substantial commissions, we bumped into Corey Bulpitt, whose work already figures prominently in our home, and we met John Brent Bennett, an incredibly talented carver and print maker. Wow! Moon over Naikoon: a bakery in a bus! These young women crank a lot of deliciousness out of this tiny kitchen. Jody, on the right, is the granddaughter of Wendy who started it all. Her grandmother was in Nanaimo when her daughter (Paige) called and told her she’d love Haida Gwaii. So Wendy packed up her Corvette Stingray and drove through a snowstorm to get here. Needless to say, the Corvette got stuck in the snow so grandma unpacked everything she could carry, plus her cat, and continued on. She made it to Haida Gwaii and started bakery. She passed away four years ago and now daughter and granddaughter come up from Nanaimo every summer to keep Moon Over Naikoon rising. Seating in the back of the bus. Perfect paint job: they had a BYOP party for their friends and community to decorate the exterior. Old Masset marina. No longer seaworthy. Jim Hart and one of his team hard at work. How’s that for a coat hook? With Corey Bullpitt, in town to collaborate with Christian White on a pole. Goodnight from the beach outside Alaska View Lodge. Oh, and one more pole to share… The end of Tow Hill Beach has the most spectacular lava rock formations, thanks to the volcano that is now Tow Hill. Easy to find Waldo… Top of Tow Hill overlooking miles of beach below. No, we did not drive our little Chevy the 25 miles along Rose Spit. Apparently, every year a few cars get washed away when the tide comes in and surprises those who don’t know how to read ride tables. Hollow log on the hike up Tow Hill. We learned that logs deteriorate from the inside out and poles that are hollowed out will stand 50-100 years longer than solid ones. Stunning green moss on the rocks. Someone can nap anywhere! …and he wanted me to do a balancing pose…I said I was already balancing on an unstable projection. Christian White’s pole at Hiellen, made from a 600 year old cedar. It’s 15.5 metres tall, not to mention the additional 3.4 metres in the ground to hold it up. It was inspired by a pole created in 1820 that fronted a chief’s house for 100 years. The original pole was painted by Emily Carr in 1911. Big trees along Golden Spruce Trail in Port Clements. The trail was originally created for people to go see the famous, 300-year-old and 50m tall unusual tree that was a symbol for many. I’m 1997, a former logger turned zealous environmental activist cut in down to protest logging. Not quite sure of the logic of that protest statement. Really big trees. Not many first growth forests around…we met a couple in here with a drone who were filming the impact of ongoing logging. They said there is a lot of clear-cutting still happening on Haida Gwaii and they want to raise awareness to stop it. Bird house community! Zoom in and look at all the different ones. Best name for a company that makes benches. Sunset at Rainbow Wharf in Port Clements. Sunshine Coast, British Columbia

Sunshine Coast, British Columbia July, 2023 Exquisite Egmont Amazing girls’ trip with my Mom and Sister-in-Law to the Sunshine Coast. What a spectacular getaway in our extended backyard! Accessible only by boat from its settling in the 1890s until the road was built 1960s, this fishing and logging community sits in a paradise of islands, coves, inlets and hiking trails.  The beautiful West Coast Wilderness Lodge was our home for three nights. Hard to leave this perfect deck and view to go exploring…incidentally, we witnessed a successful marriage proposal on this deck one evening! Logging is alive and well up here. Sad to see large swaths of dusty patches on the mountains with sections here and there of newly planted, light green trees. The warmth of a beautiful sunset from our balcony. And the serenity of the sunrise a few hours later. We are headed to Princess Louisa Inlet and Chatterbox Falls. We are ready for the adventure in our very chic expedition suits! Princess Louisa Inlet is one of the reaches off Jervis Inlet. We travelled through the deepest fjord in British Columbia (732 meters deep). Granite walls 2100 meters high, carved out by glaciers, tower over the gorge with forests and moss draped trees surrounding us. Our fabulous guide Charlie is absolutely passionate about his work and this stunning landscape he gets to live in. He told us that the original population of 36,000 Sechelt people living here was decimated by Smallpox, reduced to 106 individuals. We can’t blame this Smallpox epidemic directly on the Europeans. It came from trading routes up the coast in the 1770s, just after arm-to-arm inoculation began in 1768. Ancient pictographs are nestled in the rock faces. This one depicts an event at Deserted Bay where there was the largest population in all of Sechelt (7,000 people). This one tells the story of seals chasing fish caught in nets of the Sechelt people. Orcas came after the seals and smashed the nets. This one is of an alien. Shamans lived in these mountain and would interact with beings from another planet, so the story goes. Nobody seemed to wonder, in those days, what the Shamans might have been smoking… Apparently, when a woman from Western Australia saw this one, she said she had seen something almost identical at home. Makes you wonder how far these early peoples travelled and may have been influenced by one another. And another beautiful painting. The pigment was made from salmon eggs and arbutus bark. They have lasted thousands of years because they were painted in areas where the quartz leeches out of the rock. On this surface, the pigment stands the test of time. Chatterbox Falls is one of about 60 waterfalls in Princess Louisa Inlet. At 40m high, we were told its flow during this year’s hot summer was about 1/4 of its usual volume. Nearby is James Bruce Falls (which we didn’t get to), thought to be the highest waterfall in North America at 840 meters. Scrambling around the base of Chatterbox Falls. We were told not to climb the waterfall (as if we would)…everyone is told not to climb it due to very slippery, mossy surfaces. More than a dozen people have ignored the warnings and have died trying to climb it. Seals on a tiny, rocky island. That’s our lodge in the background. Apparently the orcas call them rock sausages… These fellas would put up a bigger fight with the orcas. A hundred or more noisy sea lions make this rocky island (and a neighbouring one) their home. There are a few bulls (the huge, light coloured ones) who have a very commanding presence, and seem to hold court with clusters of smaller ones that surround them. As we bobbed around on our boat, a number of members of the welcoming committee came to check us out, swimming around us and diving under our boat.https://videos.files.wordpress.com/yrQkEjhu/img_6719.mp4An eight km hike took us to Skookumchuk Narrows that connects the Sechelt and Jervis Inlets. Eight hundred billion litres of water flow in a tide cycle right here. The difference in water levels between one side of the rapids and the other sometimes exceeds three meters in height. Not nearly as high as what we saw in the Bay of Fundy, but this one is quite the playground for pro kayakers from around the world! Here at Roland Point, this is what these fearless kayakers do during the flood part of the cycle. In an extra large flood, which we got to witness, the water flows at 20km/hour, creating quite a ride for these kayakers. The ebb part of the cycle creates amazing whirlpools at North Point. And that wrapped our adventure in Egmont. Thanks mom, for a spectacular trip! Previous Post
